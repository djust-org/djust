- **Eager, lazy and batched LiveViews on one WebSocket are independently live (#3252).** The socket used to hold one `view_instance`: hydrating a `dj-lazy` view replaced the page view (its next click was "Event rejected"), a push aimed at one view ran on the last mounted one, and only the last view of a `mount_batch` was live. Each view a `mount` frame names with a `target_id` (the lazy hydration of the stock client, which names the container), and each `mount_batch` entry, is now a view of its own beside the page view: a full root with its own runtime (mount, authorization, state restore, explicit-exposure turns), tick task, wire version, presence, group memberships and teardown. A page `mount` (no `target_id`) and `live_redirect` still replace every view. The rest of the verbs are routed by the frame's `target_id`: `event`, `url_change`, `request_html`, uploads, presence heartbeats, and the new `unmount` (tears one view down, the others stay live). Server pushes, presence events and `db_notify` reach the views that joined the group they were sent to (`push_to_view` and presence broadcasts now carry the group), and every frame a view sends is stamped with its `target_id` (the page view's frames are unchanged). A view refused at mount (login, permission, object permission) fails alone and no longer closes the socket, an authorization refusal at event time ends that view only, and `LIVEVIEW_CONFIG["max_views_per_connection"]` (default 64) bounds how many a socket hosts. The client (`13b-view-slots.js`) addresses events and recovery requests to the view they come from, applies a slot's frames to its container against its own VDOM version, and mounts every view again after a reconnect. Over the HTTP fallback and SSE, which host one view, an event from a lazily hydrated view is refused instead of being run on the page view.
