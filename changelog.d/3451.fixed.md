- **`ManualClock.sleep` no longer loses a wake-up when `advance` runs before the sleeper's first step (#3451).** `RoomClock._wait` turns its absolute deadline into `seconds` and passes `ManualClock.sleep(seconds)` to `asyncio.create_task`; the old `async def sleep` computed `self.value + seconds` when the task first ran, so an `advance()` that ran in between (a task already ready ahead of the sleeper in the loop's queue) left the sleeper registered `seconds` past the time it was asked to wake at, and nothing woke it. `sleep()` now fixes its deadline when it is called, and a sleeper that is already due at its first step returns at once. With that window widened by an injected delay, `test_presence_timeout_retries_after_retirement_without_another_join` fails after 30 s with no tick and the log signature of the CI failures (`Clock presence-retry started`, then `stopped (graceful)` with nothing between), and passes with the fix. The natural interleaving was not observed, so that this was the cause of the CI failures is the best-supported explanation, not a proven one; the failing run's `djust timing report (#3715)` showed no stall longer than 1.3 s on the failing worker, which rules out a 30 s runner stall for that sighting. Custom `time_source` objects should measure `sleep` from the call, as documented in the shared-room clock guide. New cases: `test_manual_sleep_deadline_is_fixed_when_sleep_is_called`, `test_manual_sleep_already_due_at_its_first_step_needs_no_settle` and `test_clock_ticks_when_time_moves_between_its_wait_and_the_sleeper_start` in `test_shared_room_clock_3005.py`.
