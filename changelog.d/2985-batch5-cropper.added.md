- **ImageCropper now ships its interaction, and a validating cropper for the server (#2985, batch 5).** The component rendered a `dj-hook` that no shipped script answered. `components/static/djust_components/image-cropper.js` keeps a crop box in the image's natural pixels and lets the reader move it (drag, or the arrow keys: Shift for ten times the step), resize it (eight handles, or Alt with the arrows), draw a new one on the image, lock its shape (`aspect_ratio` "16/9", "4:3" or "1.5") and keep it above `min_width` / `min_height` (an image smaller than that is selected whole). It follows the image through window resizes, resets when the server shows another image, uses pointer events with capture (touch drags do not scroll the page), announces every change and the sent box in image pixels, and sends **only** `{x, y, width, height}` as whole numbers to `crop_event`: the image is never uploaded and no pixel is read. New `djust.components.cropping`: `crop_box` validates the numbers (the browser's claim: non-numbers, bools, `NaN`/infinity, huge ints, non-positive sizes and boxes outside the image are refused, a box that sticks out is clamped, fractions rounded) and `crop_image` (Pillow, imported on demand) refuses an image over `max_pixels` from its header before decoding, applies the EXIF orientation first (the browser shows the image upright; a box taken from the screen applied to the raw pixels crops another region), strips metadata and keeps the format. The overlay that dimmed the inside of the box is hidden (the selection's own shadow dims the outside). Markup changes are additive only (the selection becomes a labelled, focusable group with eight handles, a status region, and a new `alt` kwarg that defaults to the old text), on the component class, template tag and Rust handler alike; a byte comparison of 7,260 renders against the previous release is identical once those are removed. The script registers in `window.DjustHooks` and only when neither hook registry already holds that name. 1 component from the issue remains.
