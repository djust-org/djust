- **The kept-rows cost and the sliced-name gap of the QuerySet `.all` method read are measured and recorded (#3867).** Items 3 and 4 of the #3863 review. (3) A mixed read (rows and `.count` on one `.all`) through `{% for u in users|slice:':2' %}` still prints no count where Django prints one, on a QuerySet and a `list[Model]`, through the Django-backend engine and a LiveView: the kept rows are reached by the loop variable's alias for `users.<N>`, a filtered operand registers none, and the serialized rows carry no handle of their own. Closing it means shipping handle-bearing rows, a change to what the serializer ships, not a contained fix in the extractor or the sidecar, so it is declined and pinned by 16 strict `xfail` cells (12 are this gap; the 4 `list[Model]` cells on the Django-backend engine are attributed to #3888, where the sliced loop is empty whatever is read) plus controls that show the gap is exactly the mixed read (`python/tests/test_queryset_all_slice_3867.py`). (4) The kept rows measured at 20,000 users, release extension: render 1.19 to 1.34 s without them, 1.96 to 2.00 s with them (+48 to +69%), peak RSS 296 MB to 358 to 360 MB (about 3.2 KB a row); no cheap, obviously correct saving exists (a lazy proxy changes how the serialization floor is built), so the figure is in `docs/JIT_SERIALIZATION_PATTERN.md` instead.
