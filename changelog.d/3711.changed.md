- **No test bounds a duration with an absolute number of seconds any more, and a ratchet keeps it that way (#3711).** The sweep behind #3710 found 28 `assert`s in 20 test files that compared a measured duration with a constant (the issue named 16 lines), and two more that the clock reading reached through a helper's return value or the product's own `latency_ms`. A complexity claim ("linear on tag soup", "bounded") is now judged by growth with `djust.tests._growth.assert_scales_linearly` in `python/djust/tests/_growth.py` (four times the input must cost under ten times as much, interleaved, best of three, on this thread's CPU time), and a "did not block" claim by an ordering (a test-owned event, a frame received while another call is still blocked). `python/djust/tests/test_no_absolute_time_bounds_3711.py` finds the shape by AST (it follows `elapsed = _took(fn)` and `budget_ms = ...` to the clock, so a rename or a named constant does not hide it) and lets the per-file count only fall; its baseline is empty. Three of them were not testing what they claimed. `test_save_block_timeout_logs_and_continues_end_to_end` bounded the round trip at 2 s although the slow body it stood in for costs 0.25 s, so the bound could not see the wrapper's 150 ms timeout go; and two growth checks now fail on real behaviour and are marked `xfail` with the measurement, not fixed here: `_blank_template_comments` is quadratic on `{% comment %}` openers (`python/djust/tests/test_template_checks_skip_comments_3283.py`) and `extract_template_variables` grows about 6-8x per doubling of the template's blocks (`python/tests/test_template_variable_extraction.py`).
